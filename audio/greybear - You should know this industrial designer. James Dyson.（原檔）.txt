You should know this industrial designer. This is James Dyson, who while most consumer products were chasing new features and styling, built a career around fixing that one thing that people actually feel. Performance said the grades over time.

James Dyson is a British industrial designer, and he got fed up in 1978 when his vacuum lost suction as the bag got clogged. To fix this, he borrowed Cyclone Separating, which he saw being used in a local sawmill and started prototyping. After 5 years and 5127 prototypes, he had a working bagless prototype, but manufacturers were rejecting it.

So he went independent and launched his first mass produced vacuum, which was the DC01 in 1993. His core idea is ruthless. If the design gets worse with use, that means the design has failed.

The DC01 is clear proof of that. The dual Cyclone Tech was designed not to lose suction. Plus a clear bin that market research generally said people didn't want.

But clearly being able to see the dirt is clear evidence that the vacuum is working. He matters because he made a clear principle mainstream. Start in failure mode, engineer it out, and then make the product visible.

If you want it to follow in his footsteps, don't ask what can I add. Ask what the grades over time and design so it can't.
