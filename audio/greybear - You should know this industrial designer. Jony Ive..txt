You should know this industrial designer. This is Johnny Ive, the designer that made simplicity feel inevitable. At Apple, Ive's philosophy wasn't about minimalism.

He believed that true simplicity came from understanding complexity, then removing that fraction for the user. He put it as, true simplicity is more than the absence of clutter. If a detail didn't serve any purpose, then it was removed.

That discipline shaped products that feel obvious in hindsight. The original 2007 iPhone is a clear example of that. At a time that was quite radical, but it made the iPhone instantly intuitive.

You didn't really need instructions. You were just able to use it. The iMac G3 applied the same logic.

Translucent colors turned something that was rather intimidating into something that was meant for the home. Across the iMac, iPhone, and iPod, Ive's clearly showed that clarity meant confidence. The takeaway isn't just to make it more minimal.

It's to design with empathy, remove distractions, respect the user, and let simplicity do the work.
